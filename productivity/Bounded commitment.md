# Bounded commitment

Choice can produce anxiety because selecting one path means neglecting others, risking disappointment, and acting without certainty that the choice will become meaningful. Two opposite-looking responses avoid this exposure:

- **Commit to nothing** — use short, low-effort activities such as YouTube that end before they require a meaningful choice.
- **Commit completely** — turn an interest into an unlimited duty, so continuing feels compulsory and reconsidering it feels like failure.

Both remove ordinary agency. One continually defers choice; the other makes a choice so absolute that it never has to be made again.

## Manufactured time scarcity

Together they create the conviction that there is “no time”:

- Short, low-commitment activities consume the gaps in the day without any one episode appearing significant.
- Unlimited duties claim the remaining time in advance. Even when not actively working on them, they occupy attention and make other choices feel irresponsible.

The result is that time is either quietly consumed or psychologically pre-committed. “I have no time” can therefore mean **I have no time that feels unclaimed**, rather than that the hours are genuinely unavailable.

## Manufactured duty

A commitment may begin for good reasons but survive after its purpose has weakened. Open-ended subjects make this especially easy: there is always another tool to learn, detail to investigate, or standard to meet.

Warning signs:

- No concrete outcome depends on continuing.
- There is no definition of enough.
- Stopping feels like laziness, betrayal, or wasted effort.
- Activity creates a feeling of control without a route to an outcome.
- The project is sustained more by obligation than interest or practical value.

Past effort need not have been wasted. A useful phase can end without invalidating what it produced.

## The middle path

Choose without pretending to choose forever:

- Commit for a bounded period.
- Limit the time and energy available to it.
- Define what “enough for now” means.
- Review whether effort still connects to an outcome.
- Treat revision as judgment, not failure.
- Allow interests to remain interests rather than identities or moral duties.

The stance is:

> I choose this for now, within these limits, and I may revise the choice later.

Use this principle when setting [[NowFocus|current focuses]]. Health should remain modest, concrete maintenance; French should remain a voluntary interest rather than a new obligation.
