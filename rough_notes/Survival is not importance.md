# Survival is not importance

A useful model from the chat: the historical record passes through three filters—what gets recorded and survives, what people deliberately preserve, and what researchers choose to study. Material durability, institutional priorities, and available sources shape the result before a historian asks a question.

**Heuristic:** when something dominates the record, ask whether it mattered more or was easier to record, retain, and retrieve. Missing evidence alone cannot establish that an experience was rare or unimportant.

Study can also reinforce prominence: material that gets cited and taught becomes easier to find and more likely to be studied again. A canon partly reflects this feedback loop.

For [[Personal Knowledge Management]], a related inference is that easily captured material can crowd out fleeting thoughts. [[Preserve the Non-Obvious]] supplies the existing capture rule; this explains why relying on later retrieval can miss things worth keeping.
