# Proportionate duties to future generations

The principle explored in the chat: future people have claims on us, while present people also have claims to worthwhile lives. Being here first should not license dumping severe avoidable costs on those who cannot object.

Compare the seriousness and reversibility of the future loss with the burden of preventing it. Minor convenience is a weak reason to permit grave damage; preserving every existing natural state regardless of present cost is also too strong. Irreversibility deserves extra caution without automatically giving a loss infinite weight.

The aim is a fair inheritance of conditions and options for flourishing, not necessarily an unchanged world or the greatest possible future welfare. Some losses may be compensated by other gains; indispensable or irreplaceable goods make that much harder.

For policy calculations, distinguish discounting resources for time and opportunity cost from deciding that later people's interests matter less merely because they live later. A discount rate is a tool within a calculation, not a complete account of what is owed.

**Unresolved:** what counts as a proportionate sacrifice, and which losses cannot reasonably be substituted for? The principle frames hard cases rather than calculating their answers.
