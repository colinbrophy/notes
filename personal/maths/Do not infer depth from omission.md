# Do not infer depth from omission

A textbook not discussing a question does not imply that the question hides a deep unexplored theory.

Sometimes a direction is omitted because:

- arbitrary behaviour is possible;
- the proposed classification merely records the original object again;
- the decomposition has no constrained interaction between its parts;
- the useful cases already belong to a more structured subfield;
- the question becomes tautological once stated precisely.

General topology gives a useful warning. Arbitrary spaces allow local neighbourhood behaviour to be modified with little constraint on future modifications. Attempts to classify the resulting pathologies often reduce to bookkeeping the topology itself. Once any space can occur as the "bad fibre", there is no distinctive theory of those fibres.

Learn the standard machinery before treating omissions as research opportunities. Familiarity with constructions, invariants, universal properties, and counterexamples gives the judgement needed to ask:

- Does this construction constrain what can happen next?
- Does the invariant compress anything?
- Is the reconstruction data simpler than the original object?
- Can arbitrary objects already occur inside the proposed special case?

A mature field's standard material is often a compressed record of what proved reusable.

> Do not infer depth from omission. First learn enough machinery to distinguish fertile structure from disguised bookkeeping.
